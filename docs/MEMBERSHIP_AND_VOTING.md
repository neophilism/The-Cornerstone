# Synthetic association membership and voting

Migration 008 adds voluntary founding invitations and member decisions to the participant workspace. The adapter uses invented records only. It does not charter an association, confer legal membership, or collect official votes.

## Founding journey

Create and submit a bounded association draft referencing actual synthetic eligible claims and their owners. In this adapter the opening date must equal the submission date; backdating cannot shorten the founding period. An actual Labor publication and notice integration remains a production prerequisite. Each invited person can inspect the association and personally accept or decline its unchanged terms; an organizer cannot answer for another person. Acceptance requires an affirmative acknowledgement of equal base rights, one vote, inspection and first opportunities, without compelled work, relocation, training, capital or personal debt. Consent can be withdrawn during the published founding period. No invitation or acceptance creates a unit by itself.

An assigned Labor persona can close an ordinary roll only after the final UTC calendar date of the 180-day period, with every invitation accepted, no unresolved timely claims, eligible linked claims and the required written findings. Closing atomically creates one active unit per claim and adds the member role. It does not elevate identity assurance or confer any staff role. Personal claims and their evidence are not exposed to other members through association access.

Small cohorts may now combine across certified events. Closing derives represented events and their separate claim IDs from eligible claims, verifies every linked event and rejects an organizing event that has no actual founder. Claim identities remain intact. This snapshot does not itself establish exchange readiness or permanent priority; matching must recheck which claims remain unremedied.

Migration 009 permits larger proposed rolls while retaining the ordinary 2,500-unit ceiling. Each prospective founder may accept membership separately from a yes, no or abstention on the proposed scale. Personal choices are encrypted, commitment-bound and limited by RLS to the voter and assigned Labor reviewer; the response API returns only the caller's choice. Each change retains an immutable encrypted history and receipt. Withdrawal resets that person's scale choice to abstention. Choices may change during the founding period and stop when it closes. These choices are server-decryptable and do not promise anonymous voting.

Closing a larger roll derives the yes count from verified personal choices. More than half of all proposed founders must approve, every founder must still accept unchanged membership terms, and Labor must separately find that meetings, voting, records and direct deliberation remain practical. A staff-supplied yes count is rejected. Eligibility rows are locked in a consistent order and units are created in one atomic database operation, including the existing one-active-roll constraint. The existing one-megabyte structured-request bound is a technical adapter limit, not a new statutory membership ceiling. Post-enrollment synthetic succession is described in SUCCESSION.md; real identity and legal activation remain gated.

## Member decisions

An active founding-unit holder can draft a resolution for an accessible closed association. The server derives its unit count, fixes its association and freezes the actual unit IDs when voting opens. Every active holder in the frozen roll may cast one ballot, including a successor after the separate synthetic transfer decision. Ballot replacement is unavailable in this initial adapter.

A member sees their own choice and receipt. Shared record payloads and exports contain participation counts and salted commitments, never individual choices. Ballot bodies are encrypted and bound to the ballot, resolution and unit; choices are verified against their commitments on reading and tallying. Database policies restrict ballot choices to their original receipt actor or assigned Labor reviewer. A later holder sees only whether that unit has already voted, not the predecessor's choice or receipt. These ballots are server-decryptable, not cryptographically sealed rankings or anonymous elections; authorized reviewers can inspect them. Published tallies in a small association can reveal choices by inference.

After the closing UTC calendar date, the assigned Labor persona tallies against all frozen units. Abstentions and missing votes remain in the denominator. More than half of all units must vote yes. The tally records yes, no, abstention, uncast and total counts. It does not execute a compact, ranking, merger or financial transfer.

## Verification

`node --import tsx tests/membership.integration.ts` requires the isolated, migrated and seeded simulation database. It exercises actual event/claim findings, evidence, consent and withdrawal, elapsed founding periods, closed rolls, scopes, conflicts, duplicate units, frozen ballots, idempotent retries, private versions, immutable ciphertext and all-unit tallies. Only the in-process test harness can advance its service clock; HTTP callers cannot.

The database CI job creates one ephemeral masked field key shared by its synthetic integration fixtures and private browser server. It is discarded with the job and is never a provider credential. Browser checks cover personal consent, membership inspection, two-person voting and persisted receipts on desktop and mobile, with accessibility checks and screenshots. CI results, rather than this description, establish whether a particular commit passes.

`tests/association-scale.integration.ts` checks mixed-event identity preservation and the true 2,501-founder majority boundary. Large synthetic eligibility/response fixtures are inserted by the isolated test owner; two personal scale choices and their changes use the real service. The test rejects 1,250 yes votes, missing practical-participation findings and an official-entered count, then accepts 1,251 verified yes votes and checks all 2,501 units. It also checks privacy and transactional failure. Browser tests exercise independent consent and scale choice, changes, withdrawal and persistence. No large real-user enrollment or operational performance certification is claimed.
